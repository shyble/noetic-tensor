# Changes

## Unreleased (branch je-ops)

Additions for joint-embedding training (not yet released; no version bump). Each is tested on the CPU reference backend against f64 host references and finite differences, and on Metal (an Apple GPU, macOS) and CUDA (an NVIDIA GPU, Windows) against CpuRef, byte-identical run to run; a small joint-embedding run (set encoder, target EMA, VICReg, group InfoNCE, rank monitor, Adam) gives the same state hash twice for the same seed on each backend, and in two processes on Metal and on CUDA (each device has its own hash; never compared across devices).

- **Set attention.** `AttentionConfig::non_causal` and `masked_mean_pool` over the existing key padding mask: a set of 2–10 examples padded to a common length equals its unpadded self at the real positions within fp32 1e-6 (not to the bit: the reductions group their sums by length), padded positions get exactly zero gradient, and the pooled output is invariant to the examples' order within 1e-6.
- **`TargetEma`** (`nn::target_ema`): the target vars as a copy of the online vars, `θ′ ← τθ′ + (1−τ)θ` with `TauSchedule`, no bias correction, detached reads, save and restore of θ′, the step and the schedule. `TauSchedule::cosine(K)` is BYOL's cosine 0.996 → 1 over K updates (endpoints exact); a linear schedule is also used in published work (not built here). There is no default schedule: K is required, and K = 0 is refused. `nn::ema::Ema` is unchanged.
- **VICReg** (`vicreg`, `vicreg_variance`, `vicreg_covariance`, `VicregWeights`, default 25/25/1, γ 1, ε 1e-4), per seed, with the reference implementation's grouping (variance hinges averaged, covariance terms summed); its μ = 25 is the paper's μ = 12.5 in equation 6, which sums the two hinges.
- **Masked group InfoNCE** (`masked_group_info_nce`): variable-size candidate groups padded to K with a masked log-softmax and an optional group mask; `cross_entropy` to the bit on full groups. F16 logits are refused (the pad fill −1e9 overflows F16; padded attention should not run in F16 either). Debug builds check that each kept group's target is a valid entry (`check_group_targets`).
- **Effective-rank monitor** (`nn::rank`), host-side f64 outside autograd, with per-dimension standard deviations. Two spectra: `effective_rank` / `effective_rank_of` is Roy–Vetterli over the **covariance eigenvalues** λ; `effective_rank_singular` / `effective_rank_of_singular` is RankMe over the **singular values** σ = √λ(ZᵀZ) of the uncentred embedding matrix. They agree on flat spectra and the singular-value rank is larger otherwise; the caller chooses which one to gate on. `jacobi` reports its sweep count and whether it converged (`jacobi_eigenvalues` panics if not); a rotated d = 128 spectrum converges in 10 sweeps.

## 0.6.0

Released 15 October 2026. Three opt-in features, each off by default and each under its own key: a fast mode for data-parallel and sharded training, reduced-precision matrix products on the GPU, and mixed precision. Each is built; tested on one machine per device kind; multi-GPU, multi-machine and datacenter runs untested. The fast mode is not reproducible bit for bit. Fast, half-precision and mixed-precision results are never compared byte for byte with, or pooled with, f32 or deterministic-mode results. With every option off, the 0.5.0 results are unchanged (the gates' weights, the single-device configurations' bytes, on the CPU, CUDA and Metal).

- **Wire protocol 3.** A rank's hello says `engine=0.6.0 wire=3` (`wire_version()`; `WIRE_PROTOCOL` is 3: the fast mode's ring messages and the mode in a fast-mode rank's hello); rank 0 refuses a rank of another engine or protocol before step 0, a 0.5.0 rank (`wire=2`) included. The group key is unaffected.
- **Refusals name the key part.** A resume refused across group keys (`Checkpoint::load_latest`, `ShardedDataParallel::load_sharded_latest`) says which part differs: "another mode", "another matmul precision", or "another platform or device".

**Mixed precision** (`nn::dist::mixed_data_parallel_step`, `mixed_sharded_step`, `nn::LossScale`; opt-in; released in 0.6.0): built; tested on one machine per GPU kind (CUDA: an NVIDIA GPU on Windows, bf16 products; Metal: an Apple GPU of the Apple7 family on macOS, f16 products; up to two processes sharing the GPU; shown: one process, two-rank data parallelism and two-rank sharded training (stage 2) run twice and resumed from checkpoints with every per-step weight hash the same, the per-step loss within a tolerance of f32's over 12 steps (set with the measurement), the mixed key apart from f32's in both distributed modes, and with every option off the 0.5.0 results unchanged); multi-GPU, multi-machine and datacenter runs untested. Never compared byte for byte with, or pooled with, f32 results. See `doc/mixed_precision.md`.

- **What it does.** The matrix products at `MatmulPrecision::Half` (bf16 on CUDA's tensor cores, f16 on Metal's simdgroup matrices, f32 accumulation); f32 vars (master weights), gradients and optimizer state; a static loss scale of 2^k (exact in f32), with the optimizer step skipped on every rank when a gradient on any rank is not finite. Data-parallel and sharded, in the deterministic mode or the fast mode.
- **The identity, as far as shown.** Within the mixed key, two-rank data parallelism and two-rank sharded training (stage 2, flat) gave one process's weights bit for bit on CUDA and Metal (the gate's model, 12 steps). Other world sizes, stages and partitions under mixed precision are untested.
- **The loss against f32** over the gate's first 12 steps: within 8.04e-3 on bf16 and 1.74e-3 on f16; the tolerances 2e-2 (bf16) and 2e-3 (f16) were set with this measurement, not fixed in advance.
- **Keys.** A group made at `Half` records it and its key names the product; a step at another precision than the group's is refused (every data-parallel and sharded step checks it), and so are a mixed step on an f32 group, a job mixing precisions and a resume across precisions.
- **API** (additions): `mixed_data_parallel_step`, `mixed_data_parallel_grads`, `mixed_sharded_step`, `MixedStep`, `mixed_record`, `ProcessGroup::matmul_precision`, `nn::LossScale`, `nn::all_finite`; `ddp_gate` gains `--mixed`, `--loss-scale K` and `--zero S:P`.
- **Not built:** a dynamic loss scale, 16-bit weights or activations in memory.

**Reduced-precision matrix products on the GPU** (`tensor::MatmulPrecision::Half`, opt-in; released in 0.6.0): built; tested on one machine per GPU kind (CUDA: an NVIDIA GPU on Windows; Metal: an Apple GPU of the Apple7 family on macOS; shown: every product within the stated bound on fixed shapes, a training run repeated in a new process and resumed from a checkpoint in a new process with every per-step weight hash the same, the key apart from f32's, and with the option off the 0.5.0 results unchanged); multi-GPU, multi-machine and datacenter runs untested. Its results are never compared byte for byte with, or pooled with, f32 results. See `doc/matmul_precision.md`.

- **What it does.** Both operands of a GPU matrix product are rounded to a 16-bit float as they are loaded, and the products accumulate in f32: bf16 on CUDA's tensor cores (`mma.sync.m16n8k16`, compute capability 8.0 or later), f16 on Metal's simdgroup 8×8 matrices. One kernel per backend (64×64 tiles, k in steps of 32 in order, no split over k, no atomics), each its own source compiled on first use.
- **The option.** `set_matmul_precision` (per thread; `F32` by default) or `Tensor::matmul_with` for one product; a product's backward pass takes its precision; CPU tensors stay f32. Mixed precision (above) uses it for data-parallel and sharded training.
- **Its own key.** While the precision is `Half`, `gpu_platform_key` ends with the product's part (`-gemm-bf16-mma.m16n8k16-f32acc-tile64x64x32-kernels-<hex>` on CUDA, `-gemm-f16-simdgroup8x8-f32acc-tile64x64x32-kernels-<hex>` on Metal), so process groups and checkpoints key such runs apart; with `F32` every key is 0.5.0's.
- **Measured** (against the analytic bound, asserted by the tests): on eight fixed shapes the largest error is at most 3.6e-7 of Σ|a·b| against the exact product of the rounded operands, and within the operands' rounding against the f32 operands (largest 3.45e-3 for bf16, 4.6e-4 for f16).
- **Speed** on the two test machines, against the engine's own deterministic f32 kernels (CUDA: compiled with `--fmad=false`, one explicit fmaf per k in order; Metal: safe math mode), not cuBLAS or any other vendor library: products 2.0–3.9× (CUDA) and 1.2–1.8× (Metal) the f32 kernels' rate on eight shapes; a 16.8M-parameter decoder's training step 0.83× (CUDA) and 0.91× (Metal) of the f32 step's time. Hardware, keys and per-shape times: `doc/matmul_precision.md`.
- **API** (additions): `MatmulPrecision`, `set_matmul_precision`, `matmul_precision`, `half_gemm_key`, `Tensor::matmul_with`; the example `half_gemm_gate`.
- **Not built:** bf16 on Metal, 16-bit tensors as operands without a cast, tiles chosen by shape, cuBLAS.

**Fast mode** (`nn::dist::DistMode::Fast`, opt-in; released in 0.6.0): built; tested on one machine per device kind (the CPU reference backend on macOS aarch64 at world size 2 and on Windows x86_64 at 2 and 4; Metal with two processes sharing one Apple GPU on macOS; CUDA with up to four processes sharing a single CUDA GPU on Windows; shown: the ranks hold the same weights after every step, the per-step loss within 5e-7 of the deterministic mode's over the gate's 12 steps (a tolerance set with the measurement), a job mixing modes or buckets refused before step 0, and with the mode off the 0.5.0 results unchanged); multi-GPU, multi-machine and datacenter runs untested. Fast mode, not reproducible bit for bit. See "Fast mode" in `doc/distributed.md`.

- **What it does.** Each rank sums its own micro-steps; the gradients go in buckets (25 MiB by default, the vars in reverse order); each bucket is reduced by a ring over the TCP mesh (reduce-scatter, then all-gather; for sharded training at stages 2 and 3 the reduce-scatter alone, its segments being the ranks' pieces); with overlap on, the buckets are reduced on a communication thread during the backward pass of each rank's last micro-step, as soon as their gradients are final. Every rank still gets the same sum, so the vars stay in sync.
- **Its own key, never pooled.** The key is `mode=fast order=ring world=<W> bucket=<bytes> overlap=on|off backend=tcp platform=…`; the record ends with "(fast mode, not reproducible bit for bit)". A job whose ranks ask for different modes or buckets is refused at the rendezvous before step 0; a checkpoint of one mode is refused by the other ("another mode"); `ProcessGroup::require_deterministic` refuses the fast mode for callers that need the deterministic one. The fast CPU backend stays refused in the deterministic mode and is a device of the fast mode only.
- **The deterministic mode is unchanged** and stays the default: its sum and its key are 0.5.0's (its hello says the new versions); the 0.5.0 gates give 0.5.0's hashes on the CPU, CUDA and Metal, and the single-device configurations 0.5.0's bytes.
- **API** (additions): `DistMode`, `FastOptions` (non-exhaustive, with builders), the public field `GroupOptions::mode` and `with_mode`, `ProcessGroup::mode`, `is_fast`, `require_deterministic`. `examples/ddp_gate.rs` gains `--fast`, `--bucket-kb`, `--no-overlap`, `--losses`, `--width`, `--blocks`, `--mlp` and prints its loop's wall time.
- **Not built:** NCCL, a tree all-reduce, reduction in arrival order, compression.
- **Internal:** two message kinds on the wire (the ring's), exchanged only between fast-mode ranks; the backward pass can name each leaf as its gradient becomes final (crate-private; `backward` itself is unchanged).

## 0.5.0

**Sharded data parallelism** (`nn::dist::ShardedDataParallel`, deterministic mode only; released 15 October 2026): built; tested on one machine per device kind (the CPU reference backend on macOS aarch64 at world sizes 1 and 2 and on Windows x86_64 at 1, 2 and 4; CUDA with up to four processes sharing a single CUDA GPU on Windows; Metal with two processes sharing one Apple GPU on macOS; shown: byte-identical weights and per-step hashes to data parallelism at every stage and partition, with absent gradients, an idle weight under AdamW's decay and clipping, consolidated checkpoints byte-identical to data parallelism's, and a killed run resumed from a sharded checkpoint equal to the uninterrupted run); multi-GPU, multi-machine and datacenter runs untested. See "Sharded data parallelism" in `doc/distributed.md`.

- **Stages.** The optimizer's state (stage 1), also the summed gradients (stage 2), and also the vars between steps (stage 3) are split among the ranks, in the shape of ZeRO's stages and FSDP's full sharding; `ShardOptions` (non-exhaustive, with a builder) picks the stage and the partition: flat pieces of every var (FSDP's flat parameter, per var) or whole vars per rank (in the shape of torch's `ZeroRedundancyOptimizer`). Opt-in: nothing else changes when it is not used.
- **The same bits as data parallelism.** The reduce-scatter is the all-reduce's chain in flat (rank, micro-step) order, with only its last step cut into pieces; the optimizers update each element on its own; gathers move bits. At every stage and partition the weights and per-step hashes equal `data_parallel_step`'s at the same world size and micro-steps, with absent gradients, an idle weight under AdamW's decay, and clipping by the global norm per seed (`ShardGrads::clip_grad_norm_per_seed`, whose norms are `clip_grad_norm_per_seed`'s bit for bit). The group key is unchanged; the run's record names the sharding.
- **Checkpoints.** Consolidated checkpoints are data parallelism's checkpoints byte for byte (either kind of run resumes the other's); sharded checkpoints have every rank write and verify its own pieces under a meta file of sha256s, round-trip byte for byte, and resume a killed run to the uninterrupted run's weights; another world size, partition, key or optimizer is refused.
- **Memory.** `ShardedDataParallel::memory` reports the bytes a rank holds; on the gate's model each rank's optimizer state is 1/W of data parallelism's with flat pieces. Stage 3 saves memory only between steps: during a step every rank gathers the whole vars, so its peak memory equals data parallelism's.
- **`examples/ddp_gate.rs`**: the gates' training as an example binary the launcher starts, each rank on the device its rank on the node gives (one GPU per rank, or several sharing one); alone, it is the one-device reference with accumulation. It prints per-step weight hashes, the final weights' hash and the run record, and takes checkpoints, resume and node copies. Built; tested on one machine per device kind (ranks sharing the machine's one device: the CPU on macOS and Windows, one CUDA GPU, one Apple GPU); one GPU per rank is untested.
- **Refusals on flat pieces.** Lazy rows, multiplier tensors and per-seed learning rates need a var's shape: on a var cut into pieces each is refused when `ShardedDataParallel::new` is called, with its own error; `Partition::ByVar` keeps every var whole and takes them.
- **The record.** A sharded run's record (`ShardedDataParallel::record`) names its stage and partition (`sharding=stage-3-parameters partition=flat`); the group's key is unchanged, since the weights are data parallelism's. A later sharding mode that changes the bits gets its own key.
- **Versions at the rendezvous** (all of `nn::dist`): every rank's hello now says its engine version and wire-protocol version (`wire_version()`: `engine=0.5.0 wire=2`; `WIRE_PROTOCOL`), and rank 0 refuses a rank that says another, or none (0.4.0 and earlier), before step 0, with the reason on every rank. A 0.4.0 rank 0 refuses a 0.5.0 rank as a mixed platform. The group's key is unchanged.
- **Not built:** per-layer gathering, prefetching and overlap, offloading, mixed precision, sharding outside the flat order.
- **Internal:** `ProcessGroup` gains a reduce-scatter beside its all-reduce (one more message kind on the wire); `GradSum` and the reduce-scatter share one scaling routine. Data-parallel results are unchanged (the 0.4.0 gates give 0.4.0's hashes).

## 0.4.0

**Data-parallel ranks on a GPU** (`nn::dist`, deterministic mode only): built; tested on one machine per GPU kind (CUDA: Windows x86_64, a single CUDA GPU shared by up to four processes; Metal: macOS aarch64, one Apple GPU shared by two processes; shown: the same training twice byte-identical on the GPU, and byte-identical weights and per-step hashes for 2 and 4 ranks against 1 GPU with accumulation, with absent gradients (−0 and +0), an idle weight under AdamW's decay, gradient clipping, and a killed and resumed run under AdamW and under SGD with momentum); multi-GPU, multi-machine and datacenter runs untested. Windows ran every gate with no skips; the Mac, shared with a long benchmark, skipped the runs needing 4 processes at once. See "GPU ranks" in `doc/distributed.md`.

- **One device per rank.** `DistEnv::device(kind, ranks_per_device)` gives the rank's device from `LOCAL_RANK` (torchrun's `cuda:LOCAL_RANK`; several ranks may share a GPU); `GroupOptions::device` is the rank's device, and the data-parallel step refuses vars on another device. Gradients are copied to the host and summed by `GradSum` in the flat rank order, as in 0.3.0; every rank takes the same optimizer step on its device. No overlap or bucketing yet.
- **`GroupOptions`** gains the field `device` and is now `#[non_exhaustive]`, with `GroupOptions::new` and `with_*` builder methods: struct literals no longer compile outside the crate (breaking), and later fields will not break callers again. Its fields stay public to read and set.
- **Keys.** On a GPU the platform key gains `device=` and the GPU's platform key (`tensor::gpu_platform_key`): on CUDA the GPU model and compute capability, the driver's release and the CUDA version it supports, NVRTC's version and the kernel source's hash; on Metal the GPU, its families and architecture, the OS build and the kernel source's hash. CPU and GPU results never share a key and are never compared byte for byte; a job mixing devices is refused at the rendezvous. The CPU reference backend's key is unchanged.
- **NVML** (the driver's management library) gives the driver's release. It is loaded at run time, not linked: the `cuda` feature builds, and a process trains on one GPU, where NVML is missing; there `device_key` is an error, and a GPU key is never made without the driver's release.
- **The fast CPU backend is refused** as a device of the deterministic mode (`device_key(Cpu(Fast))` is an error); a fast mode returns later only under its own key.
- **Resume** refuses a checkpoint taken under another group key (another platform or device), besides another world size or job key.
- **SGD with momentum is checkpointed.** `Checkpoint::save` and `restore` take any `CheckpointOptimizer` (Adam or SGD); an SGD checkpoint holds the momentum buffers, and a resume into another kind of optimizer than the checkpoint's is refused, so no run resumes without its optimizer state. Adam checkpoints are written byte for byte as in 0.3.0. `Checkpoint` holds `optimizer_state` in place of `adam_m` and `adam_v`, and `CheckpointMeta` gains `optimizer` (breaking).
- **`data_parallel_grads`** returns a step's summed gradients, the same on every rank, for callers that clip them before their optimizer steps.
- **CUDA backend:** runs on `Cuda(i)`, one device per process (fixed by the first use; `Cuda(0)` by default); a device the machine lacks is refused. `Cuda(i)` for i > 0 is untested, and results that must be exact should not rely on it until a two-GPU gate passes.
- **Single-device behaviour is unchanged.** The six training configurations give byte-identical final weights and logits to 0.3.0 (0.3.0 built and run now as the reference) on the CPU reference backend (macOS aarch64, Windows x86_64), on CUDA and on Metal; the 0.3.0 CPU gates give 0.3.0's hashes. No kernel source changes.
- **`tensor::cuda::kernel_ptx_and_cubin(cc)`** (diagnostics): NVRTC's PTX for the kernel source, byte for byte the PTX the backend loads, and the CUBIN NVRTC makes for the real architecture (`nvrtcGetCUBIN`), so a GPU run can record its PTX and machine code (NVRTC's machine code, which need not be the driver's compilation of the PTX). Tested on an NVIDIA GPU.
- **Tests:** the kernel sources hold no atomic operations; `DIST_TEST_MAX_PROCS`, a development switch, lets a shared machine skip the gate runs needing more processes at once (each skip is printed).

**Tested** (measured on 0.3.0's code, before this release's changes):

- Built and tested on Linux x86_64 (CPU) and on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB and L4 (NVIDIA driver 595.91.07, CUDA driver API 13.2; NVRTC 12.8, cuBLAS 12.8.4); determinism gates passed: run-to-run identical within each GPU model and key (sm80, sm89).
- On one 200-step training probe, the CUDA trace was byte-identical on A100, L4 and the Windows host's NVIDIA GPU (two drivers, two NVRTC/cuBLAS versions). This is a measured instance, not a guarantee: other workloads are untested, and results remain keyed per GPU model and driver.

## 0.3.0

**Data-parallel training on the CPU** (`nn::dist`), with an API in the shape of torch.distributed, DDP and torchrun: a process group from torchrun's environment variables, rank-ordered collectives over TCP (standard library only, no new dependency), a data-parallel step, a sharded sampler, coordinated checkpoints and a launcher (`examples/launch.rs`). See `doc/distributed.md`.

- **Determinism.** Deterministic data-parallel training on the CPU: W processes with k accumulation micro-steps each give the weights of one process with W·k, bit for bit; shown at world sizes 1, 2 and 4 on one machine (localhost TCP). Multi-machine and GPU runs are untested.
  - Where the identity holds: weights are byte-identical across world sizes only at equal totals of global micro-steps (W·k), in the flat rank order, on one platform key.
  - The platform key includes a fingerprint of the OS maths library (libm) over fixed inputs; it is checked at the rendezvous, and a job whose ranks differ is refused before step 0.
  - A per-step weight check (`check_in_sync`) is the backstop for anything the fingerprint does not see.
  - Resume at a different world size is refused.
- **Security.** The rendezvous handshake is authenticated (HMAC-SHA256 over a per-job secret); collective traffic after it is plain TCP, neither authenticated nor encrypted: run on private networks only.
  - A multi-node job needs a per-job secret: the launch command refuses a multi-node launch without `--secret-file` (or an explicit `--no-auth`, for an isolated development network only).
  - The secret never appears in the `Debug` or `Display` forms, run records, logs or checkpoints.
- **Checkpoints:** every rank verifies the bytes rank 0 writes (sha256); resume is coordinated and refused on a key mismatch.
- **API.**
  - `NnError` is now `#[non_exhaustive]` and gains `Dist`: a breaking change for exhaustive matches, made in this pre-1.0 minor release.
  - `GroupOptions` gains public fields `platform` and `secret`.
  - Every other 0.2.0 public item and every 0.2.0 test is kept.
- **Single-device behaviour is unchanged.** Six training configurations (a decoder, a mixture-of-experts decoder and a decoder with dropout, each with AdamW and with SGD with momentum) give byte-identical final weights and logits to 0.2.0.
- **GPU sources.** No CUDA or Metal source changes in this release. CUDA was checked by a compile-only build (`cargo build --features cuda`, Windows x86_64, CUDA 12.6); nothing was run on a GPU.
- **Tested:** CPU, localhost, Mac (macOS aarch64). **Not tested:** multi-node over a real network, GPU, Linux CUDA (apart from the compile check above, which was on Windows).
